中文摘要（翻译）

基于托管的大型语言模型构建的互动智能系统不能重新训练它们所依赖的基层,因此它们的设计师在周围的架架构层上强制执行命名的认识论. 后面出现了一种困难,它得到了很少的方法论关注:当质量回归时,回归往往属于架架,而不是基板,识别它需要测量架架本身的仪器,而不是它下面的模型. 这篇文章报告了一套这样的仪器,在2026年7月17日至18日之间添加到现有宪法运行时间,并在2026年8月19日扩展到生产漂移观察器,并将其定位为可重复使用的设计模式. 该层包括六个组件经营者授权的逆境电池,作为版本回归装置,编码命名缺陷的确定性模式屏幕,作为一级对象保留故障证据的排放隔离,发出自动交付仪器健康监测器,连续集成输送三线电缆和仅用于观察的生产漂移探测器,该检测器积累了手工实验室 没有改变任何答案. 有八个可伪造的带预测 (H1到H8),每一个都以特定运行时间载体代码路径,数据库表或打字字段命名,每个都配合了明确的伪造条件;它们与伴侣架构论文的八个预测结果的关系 (F1到F8) 在专门的调整部分中列出,因为在这里报告的带是 t 结果预测依赖于该仪器的设置. 在八个中,五个 (H1,H2,H3,H5,H7) 载有在平台上的观察日期,并在专门的观察小部分中报告;三个 (H4,H6,H8) 是关于尚未发生的实验的前预测,并且在该标签下单独报告,以便没有读者错误地分配证据. 这些预测是在诚实实实验框架下进行反复,而不是作为结实的结果:电池是由平台运营商编写的,分类不是独立的,研究的平台数量是一个. 讨论指出了一个狭窄的优先权要求,区分了构成时间断言的执行点与相邻的技术,扩展了模式到学习循环的治理适应的架架必须通过测量相同的学科来测量一个执行的架,并认为读者遇到的相互作用属性,一个观察锁和每轮的来源定位, 它们是结构性强制性的.

英文原摘要

Interactive intelligent systems constructed over hosted large language models cannot retrain the substrate on which they depend, and so their designers enforce a named epistemology at the surrounding scaffold layer. A difficulty follows that has received little methodological attention: when quality regresses, the regression frequently belongs to the scaffold rather than the substrate, and identifying it requires instruments that measure the scaffold itself rather than the model beneath it. This article reports one such instrument set, added to a live constitutional runtime between 17 and 18 July 2026 and extended with a production drift observer on 19 August 2026, and situates it as a reusable design pattern. The layer comprises six components—operator-authored adversarial batteries held as versioned regression fixtures, deterministic pattern screens encoding named defects, an emission quarantine that retains failure evidence as a first-class object, an instrument-health monitor issuing autonomous handoffs, continuous-integration delivery tripwires, and an observe-only production drift detector that accumulates a hand-labelling corpus from live traffic without altering any replies. Eight falsifiable harness predictions are stated (H1 through H8), each named against a specific runtime carrier—a code path, a database table, or a typed field—and each paired with an explicit falsification condition; their relationship to the eight pre-registered outcome predictions (F1 through F8) of a companion architecture paper is stated in a dedicated reconciliation section, since the harness reported here is the instrument set on which those outcome predictions depend. Of the eight, five (H1, H2, H3, H5, H7) carry dated on-platform observations and are reported in a dedicated observed subsection; three (H4, H6, H8) are prospective—predictions about experiments that have not yet occurred—and are reported separately under that label, so that no reader mistakes the distribution of evidence. The predictions are offered for adversarial replication under an honest empirical frame rather than as settled results: the batteries were authored by the platform’s operator, classification is not independent, and the number of platforms studied is one. The discussion states a narrow priority claim, distinguishes the enforcement point of composition-time assertion from adjacent techniques, extends the pattern to the governance of learning loops—a scaffold that adapts must be measured by the same discipline that measures a scaffold that enforces—and argues that the interaction properties a reader encounters, an observation lock and per-turn provenance grounding, become testable precisely because they are structurally enforced.

英文原摘要

This essay introduces distillation laundering, a new form of regulatory arbitrage. Distillation, a technique for training large language models based on the outputs of other models instead of from scratch, is generally prohibited by the terms of service of closed-weight frontier models. However, it is economically efficient and has been key to the success of the cheap, distilled (largely Chinese) open-weight models, which have reshaped the global political economy of AI regulation. US companies have now begun to re-import the effects of distillation through various means-a process we call distillation laundering. We also discuss how attempts to lobby these models out of the market to protect closed business models face a barrier posed by their growing popularity among businesses and other users, which together form a powerful counter-interest group. Finally, we discuss potential equilibrium outcomes in AI governance and the rise of a Beijing Effect, where China's ability to shape the deployment of open models is key to the global AI race.
