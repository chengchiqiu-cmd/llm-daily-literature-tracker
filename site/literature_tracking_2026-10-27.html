英文原摘要

We study a canonical heterogeneous-agent New Keynesian (HANK) economy populated by LLM-driven households, a heterogeneous population whose consumption rules are generated by a large language model (LLM) rather than derived from optimization. We measure their consumption, saving, and expectations with designs from experimental economics. Summarized by the household's intertemporal marginal propensities to consume, the measured behavior closes the economy in general equilibrium and yields fiscal and monetary multipliers alongside the rational-expectations benchmark. The main lesson is methodological. For an LLM-driven household, how expectations are measured is part of the model. Announcing a future income change for each horizon separately leads the household to weigh the size of the change but not its timing, so that it appears myopic, whereas presenting the horizons together draws out the response to timing. In equilibrium, under joint evaluation, LLM-driven households anticipate announced policy yet overweight the near horizon relative to rational expectations, raising the fiscal multiplier above the myopic benchmark and above the constructed rational one. Both findings hold for each of three LLMs from different model families, with the size of the over-reaction varying across them. In the main experiments, removing the instruction that asks the household to weigh the timing of an announced change roughly halves the measured over-reaction and leaves the ordering of the multipliers unchanged.

小型服务企业可以购买生成人工智能工具,而不需要获得员工能力来检查其产量.因此,订阅可能是负担得起的,即使其常规使用在操作上是不切实际的.本概念论文探讨了验证工作如何改变人工智能倡议的选择. 它将资源限制和采用方式的研究与生产力和人类对自动化依赖的研究联系起来.一个透明的会计模型从毛额劳动力节约中扣除审查时间,预期纠正时间和重复成本,然后将其余的举措与共享审查能力进行测试. 在一个示范性企业中,完整报告编写提供了最大的总节约,但需要比企业提供的更长的审查时间.根据所述假设,调查编写和状态总结构成了最高可行的结合.敏感性分析显示,报告编写成为竞争力之前需要的审查改进. 这些是模型结果,而不是从业务样本的观察. 该论文提出了一个试点设计,以测量完成的工作,检查工作,并将释放的员工时间与实现的经济节省区分开来.

英文原摘要

Small service firms can purchase generative artificial intelligence tools without acquiring the staff capacity needed to check their output. A subscription may therefore be affordable even when its routine use is operationally impractical. This conceptual paper examines how verification work changes the selection of AI initiatives. It connects research on resource constraints and adoption modes with studies of productivity and human reliance on automation. A transparent accounting model deducts review time, expected correction time and recurring costs from gross labour savings, then tests the remaining initiatives against a shared review capacity. In an illustrative firm, full report drafting offers the largest gross saving but requires more review time than the firm can provide. Inquiry drafting and status summaries form the highest-value feasible combination under the stated assumptions. A sensitivity analysis shows the review improvements needed before report drafting becomes competitive. These are model results, not observations from a business sample. The paper proposes a pilot design that measures completed, checked work and distinguishes released staff time from realised financial savings. Its practical contribution is a way to make human review visible before a small firm commits to an AI subscription or custom implementation.
