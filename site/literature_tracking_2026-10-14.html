英文原摘要

IFRS S2 and Hong Kong's new climate requirements ask firms to disclose the current and anticipated financial effects of climate risk, informed by scenario analysis. Until now, research on these disclosures has been largely qualitative: content analysis, disclosure indices and counts of whether a topic is present. The numbers themselves are scattered across hundreds of report pages, tables, charts and footnotes. We use a large language model with page-cited, schemaconstrained extraction to turn them into data for 140 Hong Kong large caps over FY2024-FY2025 (267 reports, 42,192 pages). Each label is checked against the source pages under written rules. The gap is wide. Under a deliberately broad definition, which counts any climateattributed number including bounds, ranges and pilot subsets, quantified disclosure rises from 13.8% of reports in FY2024 to 34.3% in FY2025. Under a strict definition, which requires scenario-and horizon-tied effects for both physical and transition risk, it rises from 1.5% to 6.6%. Most quantified effects are bounds, ranges and ratios. Only 9 of 267 report-years contain a scenario-tied absolute amount. Markets do not visibly price the difference. Equity returns, volume, volatility and distance-to-default around report releases do not differ by disclosure level; the minimum detectable three-day abnormal return is about 1.6%. Interbank MTN spreads for the A+H and onshore-subsidiary subsample (54-62 bonds, 8-10 issuers) are uninformative, and the disclosure effect cannot be separated from the FY2025 regulatory step. Disclosure without comparable numbers is not priced at any economically moderate magnitude.

英文原摘要

Executive teams can now request an analysis through a conversational interface, but faster reporting does not automatically improve a decision. This integrative review examines the business case for language-model agents in management reporting, financial analysis, and resource allocation. It distinguishes evidence assembly, interpretation, recommendation, and authorized action. The central argument is that value depends on reducing the cost of reaching a defensible decision, including reconciliation, review, and revision. The paper contributes a decision-rights map, an accounting model of accepted-analysis cost, and a comparison of evidence requirements across decision-support products. A worked hypothetical example shows why large drafting savings can yield modest total savings. A proposed field-study design identifies observations needed to assess repeated management decisions. The article reports a literature synthesis and analytical propositions, rather than original adoption measurements or a completed organizational experiment. AI disclosure statement. Generative AI assisted substantially with literature organization, substantive drafting and revision, LaTeX preparation, and figure design. This document is a review draft and reports no new experiments or participant study. Scholarly verification and approval by the actual authors remain necessary before submission.

创建人工智能的快速增长加剧了对初级知识工作者的流离失所的担忧:随着创建模型自动化初级编码,编写和分析,下一代将如何获得领导复杂组织所需的专业知识? 同样的基于任务的自动化,使初级员工在已建立的企业层次中被移除,也大幅降低了经营成本, 最重要的是,创业本身是学习的强大石,年轻企业家通过组织运营和指导团队来获得战略判断力和管理能力. 年轻人选择要么作为初级员工,要么启动企业,要么追求外部机会.企业员工通过任务执行和管理导师来积累专业知识,创始人则通过管理自动化运营和领导项目来学习.在后期的职业生涯阶段,这种积累的专业知识被部署为企业领导或经验丰富的创业. 因此,创新人工智能具有两种相反的力量:它缩小了传统的企业学习,但扩大了创业学习.我们描述了创业学习能够完全补偿工作场所培训的侵蚀的条件. 在平衡中,自动化是否最终扩大或耗尽总体专业知识和净消费取决于新企业的学习强度以及创始人的经验在整个职业生涯中可移植性.

英文原摘要

The rapid rise of generative artificial intelligence has intensified concerns over the displacement of junior knowledge workers: as generative models automate entry-level coding, drafting, and analysis, how will the next generation acquire the expertise needed to lead complex organizations? This paper shows that this perspective overlooks a vital counterweight: entrepreneurship. The very same task-based automation that displaces junior employees in established corporate hierarchies also dramatically reduces the operating costs of running a business, lowering entry barriers for young founders. Crucially, starting a venture is itself a powerful crucible for learning, where young entrepreneurs acquire strategic judgment and managerial capabilities by orchestrating operations and directing teams. We develop a dynamic equilibrium framework in which corporate employment and entrepreneurship serve as interconnected learning environments. Young individuals choose whether to work as junior employees, launch a venture, or pursue outside opportunities. While corporate workers accumulate expertise through task execution and managerial mentorship, founders learn by managing automated operations and leading projects. In later career stages, this accumulated expertise is deployed as corporate leadership or experienced entrepreneurship. Generative AI therefore exerts two opposing forces: it shrinks traditional corporate apprenticeships, but broadens entrepreneurial learning. We characterize the conditions under which entrepreneurial apprenticeship can fully compensate for the erosion of workplace training. In equilibrium, whether automation ultimately expands or depletes aggregate expertise and net consumption depends on the learning intensity of new ventures and the portability of founder experience across careers.
