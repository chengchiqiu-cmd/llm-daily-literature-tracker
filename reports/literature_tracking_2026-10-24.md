### 中文摘要（翻译）

大型语言模型 (LLM) 引入广告的平台必须决定广告收入应该资助多少能力和消费者应该支付多少钱以避免广告.我们研究了一个断平台,在广告减少用户价值,但更好的服务提高广告回报时,共同选择昂贵的响应质量,广告强度和订阅价格. 首先,当部分支持广告和无广告的计划共存时,广告可以为纯订阅中获得无广告的消费者提供有用的服务.中间计划的有效质量较低会削弱优惠用户可用的替代方案,从而使不变的无广告服务价格更高. 一些完全支持广告的用户获得实用性,而部分支持广告的和免广告的用户损失:更广泛的访问可以与实用性极化共存. 第三,更便宜的计算可以提高质量并降低广告强度,但有足够大的成本降低可以使广告总收入高于最初水平,因为更好的服务提高了剩余的广告互动的价值. 扩展说明广告资助的升级如何使计划达到相同的最大能力和广告曝光度,消除不同价格的基础. 这些结果使菜单设计成为评估LLM广告的核心:后果超越了看到广告的用户的替代品和那些不看到的价格.

### 英文原摘要

Large language model (LLM) platforms introducing advertising must decide how much capability ad revenue should fund and what consumers should pay to avoid ads. We study a monopolistic platform that jointly chooses costly response quality, advertising intensity, and subscription prices when advertising reduces user value but better service raises advertising returns. Three findings follow. First, when partly ad-supported and ad-free plans coexist, advertising can finance useful service for consumers who receive none under pure subscription. Lower effective quality in middle plans weakens the alternatives available to premium users, allowing higher prices for unchanged ad-free service. Some fully ad-supported users gain utility, while partly ad-supported and ad-free users lose: broader access can coexist with utility polarization. Second, an intermediate advertising cap can maximize consumer surplus by preserving some revenue that finances service while limiting degradation. Third, cheaper compute can raise quality and reduce ad intensity, yet sufficiently large cost reductions can increase aggregate advertising revenue above its initial level because better service raises the value of the remaining advertised interactions. An extension shows how advertising-funded upgrades can bring plans to the same maximum capability and advertising exposure, eliminating the basis for different prices. These results make menu design central to evaluating LLM advertising: the consequences extend beyond users who see ads to the alternatives and prices faced by those who do not.

### 英文原摘要

Purpose. This review asks what the peer-reviewed evidence, read alongside intergovernmental policy and future technological advancement, permits us to say about digital learning in 2050. It examines seven technology families: artificial intelligence (AI), synthetic AI, the metaverse, extended reality (VR, AR, MR and XR), virtual training centres, the Internet of Things (IoT), and cloudedge computing with 5G and 6G networks. It also scans eleven emerging frontiers. Design. The review combines three layers. A systematic layer, reported according to PRISMA 2020, maps primary studies and reviews. An umbrella layer draws effect sizes from published meta-analyses rather than single studies. A futures layer projects the findings to 2050 through scenario analysis that extends the OECD's four scenarios for schooling. Sources span 1984-2026, with emphasis on 2010-2026, and include reports from UNESCO, the OECD, the World Bank, the ITU, the IEA, UNICEF, the European Union and the African Union. Of 1,780 records identified, 176 studies were included, 70 of them meta-analyses. Every quantitative claim was checked against its primary publication or an authoritative summary of it. Findings. The evidence base is highly uneven. Intelligent tutoring and simulation-based training have the strongest causal evidence: mean effects of roughly 0.4-0.8 standard deviations for tutoring systems, and larger effects for simulation compared with no intervention. Generative AI is promising but fragile. Randomised trials in Nigeria, Ghana and the United States report gains of about 0.3-0.4 standard deviations from well-designed tutors. A field experiment in Turkey found that unrestricted access lowered later exam performance by 17%. Augmented reality shows a medium mean effect (d = 0.68). Immersive VR shows a small one (g = 0.38), and gains depend on instructional design rather than on immersion itself. The metaverse, IoT-based learning and 6G-enabled education remain largely conceptual. Argument. The decisive variable for 2050 is not any single technology but convergence under pedagogical and public governance. Technologies amplify learning when they are bound to sound learning theory, teacher agency and equitable infrastructure. They amplify inequality and surveillance when they are not. Contribution. The review offers (i) an evidence matrix that grades seven technology families on a common scale; (ii) a five-layer convergence architecture; (iii) a horizon scan of eleven emerging frontiers with predictions for 2030, 2040 and 2050; (iv) four scenarios for 2050 with observable signposts; (v) an ethics, equity and governance analysis with specific attention to sub-Saharan Africa and Tanzania's Development Vision 2050; and (vi) a research agenda of twelve priority questions.

### 英文原摘要

We examine how richer query information changes the revenue economics of search advertising. We model the move from keyword search to conversational search as a refinement of the platform's information about query-level click-through rates and ask how this refinement affects expected revenue under different selling rules. We find that, under a fixed weighted second-price auction, finer information weakly improves matching but has an ambiguous effect on revenue. This is because more precise queries can thin out competition within realized auctions. When the platform can adapt the mechanism to the information it observes, finer information weakly raises expected revenue. With constant demand elasticities, the revenue-maximizing auction is a weighted second-price auction with tuned weights and reserves, an auction format major platforms use. Conversational search also raises questions regarding the optimal timing of ads in a conversation. We argue that a platform may prefer to wait before showing an ad, because later turns can reveal more about what the user wants, although the user may leave before then.
