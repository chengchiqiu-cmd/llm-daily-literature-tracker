## 1. 以聚合商为中心的居民能源社区计算协调：异质性学习、大语言模型与基准测试综述

### 一两句话看懂

论文综述了居民能源社区中由聚合商协调分布式能源、灵活需求、储能、电动汽车和本地能源交易的方法，重点讨论异质性学习与大语言模型（LLM）的应用。作者归纳了150篇同行评议研究，提出三类分类体系和一套比较评估框架，用于判断这些方法能否实现可靠、可复现且可持续的协调。

### 中文摘要（翻译）

居民能源社区正成为协调分布式能源资源、灵活需求、能源存储、电动汽车和本地能源交易的重要运营场景。其运行具有挑战性，因为不同家庭的资源在可用性、灵活性、行为和技术约束方面存在差异；与此同时，聚合商还必须在发电不确定、参与情况变化、网络限制和市场规则的条件下保持运行可行性。本综述综合了150篇关于以聚合商为中心的协调研究的同行评议文献，重点关注异质性学习（heterogeneous learning）和大语言模型（large language models，LLMs）作为需求响应与点对点能源协调的计算接口。论文构建了三个相互关联的分类体系。分类体系I依据市场互动、耦合关系和可行性执行，将协调范式分为集中式、分布式、分层式和交易式协调。分类体系II依据设备、行为、社会经济和约束异质性、学习架构，以及对行为漂移、季节变化、不确定性和群体变化的鲁棒性，对异质智能体学习进行组织。分类体系III将大语言模型定位为参与支持、资费与规则解释、情境感知预测和网络安全辅助的结构化运营接口，而非自主调度机制。在这些分类体系的基础上，本综述建立了一个比较评估框架，重点考察调度可行性、约束满足、可扩展性、各基准方法之间的信息对等性、分布偏移下的鲁棒性、可审计性、可复现性，以及对大语言模型生成结果的验证。论文进一步提出了可检验的研究问题，以及最低限度的报告和基准测试要求。总体而言，这些贡献为评估先进的计算方法和基于学习的方法能否支持异质居民能源社区中可靠、可复现且可持续的协调，提供了统一基础。

### 英文原摘要

Residential energy communities are becoming a key operational setting for coordinating distributed energy resources, flexible demand, energy storage, electric vehicles, and local energy exchange. Their operation is challenging because household resources differ in availability, flexibility, behavior, and technical constraints, while aggregators must preserve feasible operation under uncertain generation, changing participation, network limits, and market rules. This review synthesizes 150 peer-reviewed studies on aggregator-centric coordination, with particular focus on heterogeneous learning and large language models (LLMs) as computational interfaces for demand response and peer-to-peer energy coordination. Three connected taxonomies are developed. Taxonomy I classifies centralized, distributed, hierarchical, and transactive coordination paradigms according to market interaction, coupling, and feasibility enforcement. Taxonomy II organizes heterogeneous-agent learning according to device, behavioral, socio-economic, and constraint heterogeneity, learning architecture, and robustness to behavioral drift, seasonal variation, uncertainty, and population change. Taxonomy III positions LLMs as structured operational interfaces for participation support, tariff and rule interpretation, context-aware forecasting, and cyber-security assistance rather than autonomous dispatch mechanisms. Building on these taxonomies, the review develops a comparative evaluation framework centered on scheduling feasibility, constraint satisfaction, scalability, information parity among baselines, robustness under distribution shift, auditability, reproducibility, and validation of LLM-derived outputs. It further formulates testable research questions and minimum reporting and benchmarking requirements. Together, these contributions provide a unified basis for evaluating whether advanced computational and learning-based approaches can support reliable, reproducible, and sustainable coordination in heterogeneous residential energy communities.
